Great question! The standard quadratic formula solves any equation in the standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$:

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

For your equation $2x^2 + 5x - 3 = 0$, our coefficients are:
$\bullet\ a = 2$  |  $\bullet\ b = 5$  |  $\bullet\ c = -3$

Discriminant Insight:

$\Delta = b^2 - 4ac = (5)^2 - 4(2)(-3) = 25 + 24 = 49$. Since $49 > 0$, we know there are two distinct rational roots!
